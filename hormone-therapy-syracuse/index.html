Hormone imbalance symptoms women and men get evaluated for

Hormonal changes can affect nearly every part of how you feel, from energy and mood to sleep, weight, and libido. A hormone imbalance is worked out from your history, your symptoms, and lab work, not from a checklist on a website. But people usually decide to get evaluated after noticing changes like these, especially when several show up together and do not let up.

Common symptoms of menopause women experience

  • Hot flashes and night sweats
  • Trouble sleeping (insomnia)
  • Fatigue and low energy
  • Brain fog and difficulty concentrating
  • Poor libido
  • Weight gain, especially belly fat
  • Mood swings and irritability

Common symptoms men over 40 experience

  • Muscle loss and decreased strength
  • Difficulty focusing
  • Slower recovery from exercise
  • Poor sleep quality
  • Low energy and motivation
  • Reduced libido
  • Weight gain

These changes have more than one possible cause, and hormones are only part of the picture. The evaluation exists to find out what is going on for you, including when the answer is not hormones.

How bioidentical hormone therapy works

Bioidentical means identical to what the body makes, which makes it different from other (synthetic) forms. The pellets Medical Weight Loss NY uses are plant-based and made from organic yams, about the size of a grain of rice, and are placed under the skin in a brief in-office procedure. They release hormones over months rather than being applied every day, and every patient is dosed individually from bloodwork, hormone levels, and weight.

Which hormones

For women, depending on labs and symptoms, a plan may include bioidentical testosterone, estrogen, or both, where appropriate. If you have a uterus and take estrogen, your provider also addresses protecting the uterine lining with progesterone. For men, treatment means testosterone. Which hormones, at what dose, and whether treatment is appropriate at all are decided from your labs, your history, and your symptoms.

Why this practice uses pellets

  • Placed a few times a year, so there is no hormone cream or gel to apply each day
  • Released over months rather than applied every day
  • Dosed for you from your labs, your symptoms, and your weight, then rechecked with bloodwork
  • Placed under the skin, not taken by mouth
  • For men, injections are offered as an alternative, or as a trial before moving to pellets

Pellets are not the right option for everyone

Some people do better with a different form or route of hormone therapy, some need additional evaluation first, and some should not use hormone therapy at all. Your provider reviews your personal and family history, tells you what monitoring is required, and says plainly if another approach fits you better. If hormones are not the answer, the evaluation still tells you what your labs show and what else might explain how you feel.

Our personalized BHRT process: four steps, starting with a free call

Hormone therapy at Medical Weight Loss NY begins with a free Discovery Call, then bloodwork and a full medical evaluation in Fayetteville. Whether to treat, with which hormones, and at what dose is decided from your labs, your history, and your symptoms, never from a website quiz.

  1. Free Discovery Call, about 20 to 30 minutes

    Your first step toward personalized hormone therapy. Learn how BHRT works and what to expect, get answers to your questions, and understand what medical history and prior tests may be required. You receive your lab work script at the end of the call; no tests are needed before it. Nothing medical is decided on this call.

  2. Initial consultation and full medical evaluation, in person

    A comprehensive physical exam and a full medical history review to understand your symptoms and struggles. Your provider determines where you are in menopause (women) or andropause (men) and sets personalized dosing based on which hormones need to be adjusted according to your lab work and symptoms. You leave with a customized treatment plan tailored to your body's needs.

  3. Pellet placement, about 30 minutes, in person

    A customized hormone treatment designed for you, based on your blood levels and your health concerns. The procedure is quick, performed in-office, and requires minimal downtime. Aftercare: avoid heavy lifting, repetitive squatting, and exercise for a few days; we provide detailed post-procedure instructions. Mild swelling at the insertion site may occur temporarily. Traveling in from out of town? We may be able to do the pellet placement during your initial consultation; discuss this with the provider in advance.

  4. Recheck appointment, about 6 to 8 weeks after placement, in person

    Labs must be completed before this appointment; we provide your lab script. Your provider assesses how your body is responding and makes adjustments based on your lab results and how you feel. If a booster dose is needed, it is provided at this visit. After that, your schedule is set by your labs and symptoms: women here have typically returned about every three to five months, men approximately every six months.

Questions people ask before booking

What is bioidentical hormone replacement therapy (BHRT)?

BHRT is hormone therapy that restores hormone levels using compounds that are structurally identical to the hormones your body naturally produces. At Medical Weight Loss NY it is medically supervised care for women and men.

The process starts with a free Discovery Call, then lab work and a full medical evaluation in Fayetteville. Your provider identifies which hormones, if any, need adjusting and creates a personalized plan. The practice's preferred delivery method is pellets placed under the skin. Whether BHRT is appropriate for you is decided after your evaluation, not before.

What are the pellets made from, and how are they placed?

The pellets are plant-based and made from organic yams, about the size of a grain of rice, and are placed under the skin in a brief in-office procedure.

After numbing the area with a local anesthetic, your provider makes a tiny incision, usually just above the buttocks, slips the pellets under the skin, and closes it with a small adhesive strip; no stitches are needed. The appointment is about 30 minutes, and the placement itself takes only a few minutes. Bioidentical means identical to what the body makes, which makes it different from other (synthetic) forms.

Is BHRT for men as well as women?

Yes. Medical Weight Loss NY evaluates and treats hormone imbalance in women and in men.

For women, depending on labs and symptoms, a plan may include bioidentical testosterone, estrogen, or both, where appropriate. For men, bioidentical hormone therapy usually means testosterone delivered as pellets, with injections offered as an alternative. Men can read more on our testosterone therapy page; women in perimenopause or menopause can read more on our menopause treatment page.

Which hormones are used?

It depends on your labs and symptoms. For women, a plan may include bioidentical testosterone, estrogen, or both, where appropriate. For men, treatment means testosterone.

If you have a uterus and take estrogen, your provider also addresses protecting the uterine lining with progesterone. Which hormones, at what dose, and whether treatment is appropriate at all are decided from your lab work, your history, and your symptoms, never from a website.

How long do pellets last, and how often are they placed?

Women at our practice have typically returned for a new pellet placement about every three to five months, and men approximately every six months. The interval is individualized.

Your provider sets your schedule from your labs and your symptoms, not a calendar, and tells you when bloodwork is needed before a placement. The first recheck, with labs, is typically 6 to 8 weeks after placement.

What are the side effects of hormone pellets?

The most common effects show up in the first few weeks and are usually temporary: fluid retention, swelling of the hands and feet, breast tenderness, mood swings or irritability, acne, and hair changes.

Placement itself can cause mild swelling or bruising at the site. Less common risks depend on which hormones you use and on your own history, which is why your provider reviews your personal and family history before recommending anything and monitors your labs after you start. Call the office with anything that concerns you.

Who should not use hormone therapy?

Some people should not use hormone therapy, and some need additional evaluation or a different plan first. Your provider decides from your history, examination, and labs, and will tell you plainly.

For women, a history of breast cancer or another hormone-sensitive cancer, blood clots, a clotting disorder, stroke or heart disease, unexplained vaginal bleeding, or active liver disease generally rules hormone therapy out or calls for a different plan; starting more than 10 years after menopause, or after age 60, also calls for a more careful look at risks and benefits. For men, a history of prostate or breast cancer, plans to have children, and certain blood, heart, or sleep conditions commonly mean a different plan or no treatment.

How soon will I feel a difference?

It varies. In Dr. Scinta's experience, many patients notice a change within the first few weeks, and your provider formally assesses your response with labs at the recheck, typically 6 to 8 weeks after placement.

Everyone responds differently, and some people need a dose adjustment or a booster at the recheck before they feel their best. Results vary from person to person.
