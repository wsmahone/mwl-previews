Is This Program Right for You?

GLP-1 and related weight-loss medications may be appropriate for adults who have struggled to lose weight with nutrition and exercise alone and who meet the medical criteria for the medication being considered. A full medical evaluation makes the decision.

This program may fit people who:

  • Have tried multiple diets or exercise plans and repeatedly regained weight
  • Meet FDA-labeled weight criteria for an approved weight-management medication
  • Have weight-related conditions such as prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or sleep apnea
  • Are already taking a GLP-1 through an online service and want local medical follow-up, laboratory review, and body-composition monitoring. If you want to keep your current prescriber and add our support, see the Outside GLP-1 Optimization Program.
  • Previously tried a GLP-1 but stopped because of side effects, cost, access problems, or a plateau

GLP-1 treatment may not be appropriate for everyone.

Important considerations include pregnancy or plans for pregnancy, a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2, certain significant gastrointestinal conditions, and other medical factors. A history of pancreatitis and other risk factors also require careful clinical review. Your provider will review your medical history, medications, and laboratory information before making a recommendation.

If you simply want medication without the evaluation, nutrition plan, and follow-up, this is probably not the right program for you.

How the Program Works

  1. Free Discovery Call

    An introductory call of up to 30 minutes with our team. We explain how the program works, answer general questions, and review pricing. Medical decisions are not made on this call.

  2. Testing and bloodwork

    Your first office visit includes testing and laboratory work. Recent outside laboratory results may be reviewed when appropriate, and recent EKGs may be accepted based on our current clinical policy.

  3. Provider visit

    A Medical Weight Loss of New York provider reviews your labs, medical history, medications, weight history, and previous treatment attempts. Together, you discuss whether a GLP-1, another medication, or a non-medication approach is most appropriate.

  4. Personalized nutrition plan

    You meet with our nutrition team and leave with a plan designed around your needs, including adequate protein and other strategies intended to support fat loss while helping preserve lean mass. Meal replacements are optional.

  5. Start low and adjust as you go

    If a GLP-1 or related medication is prescribed, dosing generally begins low and is adjusted gradually according to the specific medication, your response, and tolerability. Follow-up visits track weight, body composition, side effects, and overall progress.

  6. Maintenance

    As you approach your goal, treatment shifts toward maintaining your progress. Your provider continues to adjust medication, nutrition, exercise, and follow-up based on your individual needs.

GLP-1 and Related Weight-Loss Medications

GLP-1 medications act on pathways involved in appetite, fullness, digestion, and blood-sugar regulation. Tirzepatide is a related medication that acts on both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. These medications can substantially reduce hunger and food intake for many patients, making it easier to follow a nutritional plan.

But less hunger does not automatically create good nutrition, preserve lean mass, change eating behaviors, or create a long-term maintenance strategy. That is why the care around the medication matters.

Semaglutide

Wegovy (semaglutide) is FDA-approved for chronic weight management in appropriate patients and is now available in both injectable and oral tablet forms. Semaglutide is also marketed under other brand names for different indications, including type 2 diabetes.

Tirzepatide

Zepbound (tirzepatide) is FDA-approved for chronic weight management in appropriate patients. Tirzepatide activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors and is administered by injection. It is also marketed under another brand name for type 2 diabetes.

Which medication is right for me?

There is no single medication that is best for everyone. Your health history, other medications, prior response, side effects, treatment goals, insurance coverage, cost, and personal preferences can all affect the choice. Your provider evaluates the whole picture before recommending treatment.

Can weight-loss medications be combined?

In selected patients, our providers may consider another anti-obesity medication as part of an individualized treatment strategy. Combination treatment is not automatically appropriate and is considered case by case based on the medications involved, your medical history, and your response to treatment.

Protecting Lean Muscle During Weight Loss

When people lose a significant amount of weight, some of the weight lost can come from lean tissue as well as body fat. Our goal is to minimize unnecessary lean-mass loss while improving overall health and body composition.

  • Adequate protein intake
  • Appropriate overall nutrition
  • Resistance exercise when medically appropriate
  • Body-composition monitoring
  • Regular assessment of medication effect and appetite suppression

The scale tells us how much you weigh. It does not tell us what you are losing.

What If I've Already Tried a GLP-1?

A disappointing first experience does not necessarily mean every GLP-1 strategy will fail. We look at the entire treatment plan, including the medication, dose, duration of treatment, side effects, hunger control, nutrition, protein intake, body composition, other medications, medical conditions, and quality of follow-up.

Sometimes the issue is not simply the medication. It is what was missing around the medication.

Questions People Ask Before Booking

How much does the GLP-1 weight-loss program cost?

Program pricing is reviewed during the free Discovery Call before you schedule the medical evaluation.

Medication cost depends on the medication and form prescribed, insurance coverage, and other factors. Our office is self-pay and out-of-network; current payment options can be reviewed on the call.

Does insurance cover GLP-1 medications for weight loss?

Sometimes. Coverage varies widely by insurance plan and by the specific medication and indication.

Our office does not bill insurance for visits, but a superbill may be provided when applicable. Patients should confirm medication coverage directly with their plan.

Semaglutide or tirzepatide: which is better for weight loss?

Both can be effective.

Tirzepatide and semaglutide act on different hormonal pathways and have different dosing, side-effect, cost, and coverage considerations. The better choice is the one that fits your medical history, response, tolerance, coverage, and goals.

Who qualifies for a GLP-1 weight-loss medication?

Eligibility depends on the FDA labeling for the specific medication and your individual medical history.

For commonly used chronic weight-management medications, adult criteria generally include obesity or overweight with at least one weight-related condition, but qualifying on paper does not automatically mean a medication is right for you.

What are the common side effects?

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal, including nausea, diarrhea, constipation, vomiting, and abdominal discomfort.

Side effects are more likely during treatment initiation or dose escalation. Your provider reviews risks, precautions, and warning symptoms before and during treatment.

Do I need lab work before starting?

Our program includes medical evaluation and laboratory review before prescribing.

The exact tests and how often they are repeated are determined by your provider based on your history, medications, and treatment plan.

How often will I come in?

You begin with the evaluation process and then have regular follow-up visits.

Visit frequency depends on your medication, dose changes, side effects, progress, and provider recommendations. Follow-up is typically closer during periods of active medication adjustment.

Can visits be done by telehealth?

Telemedicine may be available for appropriate follow-up visits, but testing, laboratory work, body-composition measurement, and other parts of the program require in-office care.

Your provider determines which visits can be completed remotely.

How long will I stay on a GLP-1?

There is no single duration that fits everyone.

These medications are used for chronic weight management, and the treatment plan is reassessed over time based on effectiveness, side effects, cost, medical appropriateness, and maintenance needs.

What happens if I stop taking a GLP-1?

Appetite often increases after treatment is stopped, and weight regain can occur.

That is why maintenance planning is part of our program. Any decision to reduce, change, or stop medication should be made with your provider rather than treated as an abrupt end to weight-management care.

What is the difference between brand-name and compounded GLP-1 medication?

FDA-approved products have been reviewed for safety, effectiveness, and manufacturing quality.

Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and may only be compounded and prescribed when applicable legal and clinical requirements are met. If a compounded medication is being considered, your provider will explain the reason and the differences.

How is a GLP-1 different from phentermine?

Phentermine is an oral sympathomimetic appetite suppressant.

GLP-1 medications act on hormonal pathways involved in appetite, fullness, digestion, and blood-sugar regulation. The safety profile, cost, dosing, and expected response differ, so the right choice depends on the individual patient.

Will I lose muscle on a GLP-1?

Significant weight loss can include some loss of lean tissue.

We address this with adequate protein, appropriate nutrition, resistance exercise when medically appropriate, body-composition monitoring, and medication adjustments when appetite suppression becomes excessive.

I am already on a GLP-1 from an online service. Can you take over my care?

Potentially, yes.

Bring your current medication information, dose history, and recent laboratory results. Our provider will complete the appropriate evaluation and determine whether continuing, adjusting, or changing your current treatment makes sense.

If you want to keep your current prescriber and add our support, see the Outside GLP-1 Optimization Program.

What is the difference between the free Discovery Call and the medical evaluation?

The Discovery Call explains how the program works and what it costs. It is not a medical visit.

The medical evaluation is the next step if you choose to proceed and includes the clinical assessment needed to determine whether medication is appropriate.

Can my spouse or partner join the Discovery Call?

Yes.

You are welcome to include a spouse, partner, or other person you choose on the introductory call. We also see patients from throughout Central New York and Upstate New York.
