The MWL difference

We don't sell one weight-loss program

We determine what may be preventing you from losing weight and build the treatment around you.

One person may struggle primarily with hunger. Another may be a night eater or frequent snacker. Someone else may have difficulty planning meals, may need more structure, or may have medical conditions or medications that affect their weight.

That is why we start with a clean slate. We look at the whole person, identify the factors that may be contributing to weight gain or making weight loss more difficult, and then determine which tools make the most sense for you.

Your treatment plan may include nutritional changes, behavioral strategies, exercise guidance, prescription weight-loss medication, meal replacements, or a combination of approaches. And as your body, health, and needs change, your plan changes with you.

Do I Have to Take a GLP-1 to Lose Weight?

No.

GLP-1 medications can be extremely useful for appropriate patients, but they are not the only way to achieve meaningful weight loss.

Some patients may benefit from a GLP-1 medication. Others may do very well with phentermine or another oral medication. Some patients may not need prescription weight-loss medication at all.

At Medical Weight Loss of New York, we do not start with a particular drug and try to fit you into that treatment. We start with you. Then we determine which tools are most likely to help you succeed.

The Four Pillars of Our Weight Loss Program

Our personalized approach is built around four important areas of obesity medicine: nutrition, medical management, behavioral management, and exercise.

Nutrition

There is no perfect diet. Our team works with you to find an approach that fits your lifestyle rather than forcing your lifestyle to fit a particular diet.

Medical Management

Medication is not the program. It is one tool that may be used as part of a much broader medical weight-loss strategy.

Behavioral Management

Knowing what to eat is only part of successful weight management. How, when, and why you eat can be just as important.

Exercise

Exercise is important for overall health and long-term weight management, but more exercise is not always better, especially at the beginning of a weight-loss program.

Questions patients ask before scheduling

Will I have to follow a specific diet?

There is no perfect diet. The best nutritional plan is one that helps you lose weight, supports your health, and is realistic enough to follow in your everyday life.

You may have done well with a low-carbohydrate or keto-style plan in the past but no longer have the time to shop, cook, and plan every meal. In that situation, a more structured program or the use of meal replacements may make more sense. Other patients may prefer a food-based plan using meals they prepare at home.

Will I be prescribed weight-loss medication?

For some patients, lifestyle changes alone are not enough. Prescription weight-loss medications can be an important tool when they are medically appropriate.

Depending on your health history, goals, previous treatment, hunger levels, and other factors, your provider may discuss options such as GLP-1 medications, phentermine, or other oral weight-loss medications. Your provider will help determine whether medication is appropriate for you, which option makes the most sense, and how it should be incorporated into your overall treatment plan.

What if my problem is snacking, night eating, or stress eating?

These behaviors are not all treated the same way.

Some people struggle with frequent snacking. Others eat most of their calories at night, experience episodes of overeating, eat in response to stress, or have difficulty controlling hunger in certain situations. We work to identify your individual eating patterns and develop strategies specifically for the problems that are getting in your way. Depending on the situation, treatment may involve behavioral strategies, nutritional changes, medication, or a combination of approaches.

How much exercise will I need to do?

For some patients, suddenly increasing exercise can substantially increase hunger and make it harder to stay on their nutritional plan.

We help you develop an activity strategy based on your current fitness level, health, hunger response, and stage of weight loss. As you progress and move closer to weight maintenance, exercise can be gradually increased to help improve fitness, support lean mass, preserve your progress, and support long-term health.

Does my plan change as I lose weight?

A successful weight-loss program should not remain exactly the same from beginning to end.

Your hunger can change. Your nutritional needs can change. Your medications may change. Your exercise capacity improves. Strategies that worked during the first few weeks of weight loss may not be the same strategies you need six months later. That is why ongoing follow-up is such an important part of our approach. We monitor your progress, identify problems early, and adjust your plan along the way to help you continue moving toward your goals.

What happens when I reach my goal?

When you reach your goal, the focus changes from losing weight to maintaining it.

Long-term weight maintenance is not an afterthought. It is part of the plan from the beginning.

What if I have already tried diets, programs, or medications?

For more than 20 years, our team has helped thousands of patients throughout Syracuse and Central New York who have struggled with their weight, often after trying multiple diets, exercise programs, medications, or weight-loss plans without lasting success.

Whether you have struggled with weight for several years or for most of your life, we want to understand what you have already tried, what worked, what did not, and what may help you succeed now.

What if I want to lose 100 pounds or more?

Whether you want to lose 30 pounds, 50 pounds, 100 pounds or more, our goal is not simply to help you lose weight.

We want to help you improve your health and develop a realistic strategy for maintaining your progress long term.
