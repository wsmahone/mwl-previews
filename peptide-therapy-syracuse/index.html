Is peptide therapy right for you?

Peptide therapy at Medical Weight Loss NY is for adults dealing with slow recovery, stiffness, low energy, or the visible signs of aging who want a physician-guided protocol rather than a product ordered online. Whether it fits you, and which protocol, is decided at a medical consultation, not on this page.

The concerns people bring to a peptide consultation here

  • Joint pain, stiffness, or slow recovery after workouts, an injury, or surgery
  • Low energy or fatigue that has not responded to sleep, diet, and exercise
  • Difficulty building muscle, or recovery that takes longer than it used to
  • Skin aging, fine lines, thinning hair, or brittle nails

What peptides are, and what they are not

Peptides are short chains of amino acids, the building blocks of proteins. Your body makes many of them as signaling molecules; insulin and GLP-1 are peptides. Peptide therapy uses specific peptides, given in a set protocol, with the goal of supporting a process such as tissue repair, collagen production, or energy metabolism. It is not a cure for anything, and it is not the same as hormone replacement or weight loss medication.

What peptide therapy is here

  • A medical program. Every protocol starts with a 45-minute consultation with a provider and is checked at two weeks and four weeks.
  • Physician guided. Dr. Scinta holds a certification in peptide therapy from the American Academy of Anti-Aging Medicine and the International Peptide Society, and protocols are built around your goals, symptoms, and history.
  • Priced in the open. Five protocols, $196 to $446 a month, published before you call.
  • Explained. The specific peptides in your protocol, how they are taken, and what is and is not known about them are covered at your visit.

What it is not

  • Not FDA-approved. Most peptides used in this field are compounded, and compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. The FDA does not check their safety or effectiveness before they are sold, and for many of them human safety data are limited. We do not call them approved. See the FAQ below.
  • Not a cure. Protocols are designed to support recovery, energy, and appearance. They do not treat or cure arthritis, long COVID, POTS, eczema, psoriasis, acne, or any other diagnosed condition, and they are not a substitute for that care.
  • Not GLP-1 weight loss medication. GLP-1 medications are FDA-approved peptide drugs for weight management and belong to a separate program with its own evaluation.
  • Not a website that ships vials. Buying peptides online with no prescriber means no one has examined you, no one is checking your response, and no one is accountable for what is in the vial.

Questions people ask before booking

What is peptide therapy?

Peptide therapy is the use of specific peptides, short chains of amino acids that act as signaling molecules in the body, in a set protocol aimed at supporting a process such as tissue repair, recovery, collagen production, or energy metabolism.

At Medical Weight Loss NY it is delivered as a physician-guided program: a 45-minute medical consultation, a protocol chosen from five published programs, a phone consultation at two weeks, and a reevaluation at four weeks. It is not a cure for any condition, and results vary.

Are peptides FDA-approved?

Most are not. A few peptides are FDA-approved prescription drugs, including insulin and the GLP-1 weight-loss medications. The peptides used in recovery, skin, and energy protocols are usually compounded, and compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. The FDA does not check their safety or effectiveness before they are sold.

The FDA's position is moving. In 2023 it flagged many of the peptides marketed for recovery and healthy aging as compounding ingredients that may present significant safety risks, citing possible immune reactions and limited human safety data. It has since moved several of them to a nominated-but-withdrawn list after their nominations were withdrawn, and on July 23 and 24, 2026 its Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee met to consider whether seven of them should be added to the list of substances pharmacies may compound. Advisory committee recommendations are not binding on the FDA, and those are steps in a review, not approval. As of September 2026, none of them is an FDA-approved drug. Sources: FDA, bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks and FDA, Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meeting, July 23 to 24, 2026.

We do not describe any protocol as FDA-approved. At your consultation your provider tells you the status of each peptide in your protocol, and what is and is not known about it, before you decide. Results vary.

How are peptides taken?

If needles are a concern, say so at the visit. Your provider will tell you what your options are for the goal you have.

What are the side effects of peptide therapy?

Side effects depend on the peptide. Commonly reported ones include irritation or redness at an injection site, headache, flushing, nausea, fatigue, and water retention. Some peptides can affect blood sugar or appetite, and any injected peptide carries a risk of an immune reaction.

Because most of these peptides have limited long-term human safety data, unknown risks are part of the honest picture. That is one reason the two-week and four-week checkpoints exist. Report anything unexpected to the office rather than waiting for your next visit.

Is peptide therapy safe?

No peptide protocol is risk-free, and the practice does not describe any protocol as safe in absolute terms. Most peptides used in recovery, skin, and energy protocols are compounded, are not FDA-approved, and have limited long-term human safety data, so unknown risks are part of the honest picture.

That is why every protocol here starts with a medical consultation with a provider and is checked at two weeks and four weeks, and why anything unexpected should be reported to the office rather than waiting for the next visit. Commonly reported side effects are covered in the side-effects question in this FAQ.

Who should not use peptide therapy?

Anyone who is pregnant or breastfeeding, anyone with an active cancer or a history of cancer, and anyone hoping to treat a diagnosed disease with peptides instead of standard care should not start a protocol without that conversation first. Other health conditions and medications can rule out specific peptides.

Your provider decides based on your history and will tell you plainly if peptide therapy is not appropriate for you.

How is peptide therapy different from GLP-1 weight loss medication?

GLP-1 weight-loss medications are peptides, but they are FDA-approved prescription drugs for weight management and type 2 diabetes, and at our practice they belong to the GLP-1 weight loss program, with its own lab work and evaluation.

The protocols on this page are aimed at recovery, energy, skin, gut, and post-illness fatigue, not weight loss. If weight is your main goal, start with the GLP-1 program or the medical weight loss program instead.

How is peptide therapy different from hormone therapy?

Hormone therapy replaces a hormone your body is low on, such as testosterone or estrogen, with dosing set from your blood levels. Peptides are signaling molecules used to support a process such as repair or recovery, and they are not hormone replacement.

Some people do both under one provider. Hormone therapy at our practice has its own evaluation and pricing; see Hormone Therapy (BHRT).

Do I need lab work before starting peptide therapy?

Not always. The consultation covers your goals, symptoms, and history, and your provider decides whether labs are needed for the protocol being considered.

Bring any recent bloodwork you have. It helps your provider and may save you a draw.

Can I combine two stacks?

Sometimes, and only under your provider's direction. Several protocols overlap in what they support, and combining them may not be worth the extra cost.

If you have more than one goal, say so at the consultation. Your provider will sequence or combine protocols and tell you the price before anything starts.
